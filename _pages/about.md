I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Government at Cornell University, specializing in Comparative Politics (major field), Methods, and Political Thought (minor fields).

My research integrates comparative politics, political economy, and behavioral sciences, informed by political theory and intellectual history. In my dissertation book project, I develop a behavioral political economy framework to study political disobedience. Specifically, I investigate the chain of psychological components—from early family experiences to personality and cognitive-motivational states that generate political dissent—and how these components interact with political institutions, culture, and historical legacies. Beyond this core agenda, I maintain broader interests in political psychology, behavior, and the political economy of development. I am also engaged with metascientific questions related to research replicability and knowledge accumulation in the social sciences.

Methodologically, I primarily employ quantitative and computational approaches while integrating qualitative insights when appropriate. I have particular interests in survey methodology (e.g., preference elicitation on sensitive topics, multimodal data analysis including text, audio, and behavioral traces) and lab-in-the-field measurement of political behavior (e.g., mouse- and eye-tracking and electrodermal activity). Recently, I have been exploring the development of AI-powered pipelines to analyze qualitative data. I also work on broader problems in causal inference and experimental design, including site selection, factorial and adaptive experiments, and other questions in econometrics, psychometrics, GIS, and machine learning.

At Cornell, I am a graduate fellow at the Institute for European Studies and the Center on Global Democracy, an affiliate of the Cornell Center for the Social Sciences, and a research assistant for the Russian Election Study. Prior to Cornell, I worked as a research assistant at the Center of Institutional Studies (now the Laboratory for Institutional Analysis of Economic Reforms) at HSE University and was a resident at the Digital Humanities Center at ITMO University. I have also contributed to various academic programs as a lecturer and teaching fellow. I hold a Master's degree in Comparative Social Research and a Bachelor's degree in Political Science from HSE University, with a minor in Data Science and research specialization in formal and quantitative methods. I was a visiting student at the Department of Social and Behavioral Sciences at Leiden University (The Netherlands) in Fall 2019. My work has been supported by the Cornell Einaudi Center for International Studies, the Roper Center for Public Opinion Research, and the Institute for Humane Studies.
